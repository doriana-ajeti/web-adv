Roses are among the most iconic and widely recognized flowers in the world.
Known for their rich fragrance and layered petals, they come in a variety of colors,
each carrying its own meaning—red for love, white for purity, and yellow for friendship.
Roses have been celebrated in literature, art, and culture for centuries, often symbolizing beauty and romance.
They are also cultivated for perfumes, essential oils, and even culinary uses, such as rose water and rose hip tea.

Tulips are elegant, cup-shaped flowers that bloom in the spring, often seen in vivid shades like
red, yellow, pink, and purple. Originally from Central Asia, they became especially popular
in the Netherlands, where tulip cultivation turned into a major industry and even led to "Tulip Mania" in the 17th century.
Tulips symbolize renewal and the arrival of spring, and they are often associated with love and cheerful thoughts.
Their simple yet striking shape makes them a favorite in gardens and floral arrangements.

Sunflowers are tall, bold blooms known for their large yellow petals and dark centers, which resemble the sun.
Native to North America, sunflowers are not only beautiful but also useful, providing seeds for food and oil.
They symbolize loyalty, warmth, and positivity, often turning their heads to follow the sun—a behavior known as heliotropism.
Sunflowers thrive in open, sunny environments and are a popular choice in both wildflower gardens and agricultural fields.

Lilies are graceful flowers with large, trumpet-shaped blooms and a sweet, sometimes intense fragrance.
They come in various colors, including white, pink, orange, and yellow, and each variety carries its own symbolism.
For example, white lilies often represent purity and are commonly used in weddings and funerals.
Lilies are found in many parts of the world and are admired for their beauty and elegance,
often blooming in late spring to early summer and thriving in well-drained soil.
